## What banks usually ask for
The HKMA says there is no single checklist: each bank decides based on its own policy and risk assessment. Banks generally ask for proof of identity (such as a Hong Kong identity card or travel document) and address information; in some cases a bank may ask to verify your address and should explain why. Banks may also ask for other information depending on the case, such as tax information related to FATCA and AEOI. Check with the bank or its website first, and compare a few banks.

## What banks should not do
The HKMA says banks should not refuse an account without doing customer due diligence; should not make unreasonable requests that are irrelevant or out of proportion to the risk assessment; and should not require you to buy wealth management, investment or insurance products or make a large initial deposit as a condition of opening an account, or link these to your chances of success or the processing time.

## HKU's advice
The HKU handbook suggests that non-local students open a local bank account after arriving and transfer money into it: ask your overseas bank to remit money to your Hong Kong account, or deposit traveller's cheques or bank drafts into a local account (personal cheques may take up to three weeks to clear). Students under 18 must be accompanied in person by a parent or legal guardian to open an account; the University cannot act as guardian.

## Avoid scams
The HKMA "Smart Consumers" pages include "Don't Lend/Sell Your Account" and anti-fraud advice. Never let anyone else use your account.
