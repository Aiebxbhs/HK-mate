## Themed streets by what you want
Electronics: Apliu Street, Sham Shui Po (new and second-hand electronics; open later on weekend and holiday evenings). Sneakers: "Sneakers Street" (Fa Yuen Street), Mong Kok. Kitchenware: Shanghai Street. Toys and collectibles: Tai Yuen Street ("Toy Street"), Wan Chai. Antiques: Cat Street (Upper Lascar Row), Sheung Wan. Chinese medicine and dried seafood: Ko Shing Street, the dried seafood stretch of Des Voeux Road West, and Wing Lok Street, Sheung Wan. Flowers: Flower Market, Prince Edward. Goldfish: Goldfish Market, Mong Kok. Jade: the Jade Market and Canton Road.

## Street and night markets
Ladies' Market, Mong Kok: about a kilometre of open-air stalls selling clothes, accessories, knick-knacks and souvenirs; bargaining is common. Temple Street Night Market, Yau Ma Tei: souvenirs, crafts, electronics and dai pai dong food stalls, with fortune tellers and Cantonese opera in the evening. Jardine's Crescent, Causeway Bay: a covered street market mainly for clothes and accessories near Causeway Bay MTR station; the nearby Island Beverley mall has many Japanese and Korean fashion shops.

## Open late
apm mall in Kwun Tong stays open late (restaurants close around 11 pm). The DON DON DONKI branches in Causeway Bay and Tsim Sha Tsui are open 24 hours, as is the Wellcome on Great George Street, Causeway Bay; 7-Eleven and Circle K convenience stores are everywhere. Many Sasa branches open until 10 pm.

## Good to know
Opening hours and shops change, so check the latest information before you go. Bargaining is normal at market stalls; check whether electronics are new or used and what warranty they have before buying.
